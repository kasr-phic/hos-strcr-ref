The legal foundation

UHC Mandates

Republic Act 11223, the Universal Health Care Act, signed February 2019, sets out what DOH and PhilHealth are each individually responsible for — and where the law requires them to act together. Everything in the other three tabs sits on top of this division of labor.

For DOH — population-based health services

Sole responsibility

Health system stewardship

DOH runs the parts of the system that serve whole populations rather than individual claims: disease surveillance and outbreak response, health promotion, regulation of facilities and drugs, and the buildout of the hospital and primary-care network itself.

  • Contracts and oversees province-wide and city-wide health systems (through CHDs and LGUs)
  • Sets and enforces facility licensing levels, drug price references, and procurement mark-up ceilings
  • Owns the capital side — building, upgrading, and staffing DOH-retained hospitals
  • Runs MAIFIP, its own last-resort financial assistance fund for costs PhilHealth doesn't cover
Governance role

Chairs the machinery

The Secretary of Health sits as ex-officio chair of the PhilHealth Board and the Boards of Trustees of all four GOCC specialty centers — DOH's stewardship role extends into governing the financing side, not just delivering care directly.

For PhilHealth — individual-based health services

Sole responsibility

National purchaser

Since the UHC Act, every Filipino is automatically a PhilHealth member — direct or indirect contributor. PhilHealth's job is to buy care on their behalf: setting case rates and benefit packages, contracting provider networks, and paying claims.

  • Contracts public, private, or mixed provider networks for individual-based services
  • Sets case rates, Z-Benefit packages, and Specialized Outpatient (SO) Benefit packages
  • Runs a quality-and-safety rating system that can recognize third-party accreditation for incentive purposes
  • President and CEO is a presidential appointee, drawn from the PhilHealth Board
Financing role

The premium pool

PhilHealth's compulsory-contributory share of national health spending has grown from 10.1% (2023) to 19.5% (2025) — see DOH Hospitals tab for how that plays out at the facility level, and Delivering on Mandates for where that financing still overlaps with DOH's own.

Shared — where the law requires joint action

RA 11223, Sec. 42–43

Joint rulemaking

DOH and PhilHealth are required to jointly issue implementing rules and regulations, in consultation with the UHC Coordinating Council — the standing structure Congress built specifically because neither agency can execute UHC alone.

Special Health Fund

Where the money actually meets

Income LGUs receive from PhilHealth payments flows into the same province-wide or city-wide Special Health Fund that DOH helps set guidelines for — the single clearest point in the law where DOH's population-based mandate and PhilHealth's individual-based mandate are meant to reconcile into one pool, not two.

Operating philosophy for this shared work

Two principles govern how the DOH–PhilHealth working relationship approaches the mandate above, drawn from the joint working agenda between the two agencies.

Komorebi
Japanese — sunlight filtering through the leaves of a forest canopy

Used here as a working metaphor: light reaches the forest floor first wherever the canopy is thinnest — wherever alignment, traction, or precedent already exists. The two principles below translate that into how DOH and PhilHealth prioritize joint work under a narrowing window before the current term ends.

1 — Synergy and alignment are paramount

DOH and PhilHealth workstreams move as one agenda, not two parallel processes — shared standards, shared asks, shared timelines. See Delivering on Mandates for where that alignment is still incomplete.

2 — Leverage partnerships and existing traction

Where a facility, program, or financing vehicle already has momentum — YAKAP, a World Bank loan, a hospital already mid-upgrade — reposition it rather than starting from scratch. The thinnest canopy is wherever traction already exists.

Strategic focusShift to Global Budget; initiate DRGs; address Z-Ben contracting bottlenecks; expand YAKAP × GAMOT inclusions; clear HRH incentives via SHF guidelines. Also the addressee of an 8 Jul 2026 HPAAC objection letter — see below.
HPAAC objects to the contribution-linked incentive proposal — 8 Jul 2026

Position. HPAAC states it "fully supports PhilHealth's efforts to improve premium collection among direct contributors," but requests, "with all humility," that PhilHealth withdraw the proposal.

Concern 1 — inequity. Two patients with the same illness, receiving the same care in the same hospital, would receive different levels of PhilHealth support — inconsistent, HPAAC argues, with the equity and solidarity principles underpinning the UHC Act.

Concern 2 — counterproductive incentive. Members may come to see premium payment as a voluntary purchase of enhanced benefits rather than a continuing obligation. A 20% increase in all case rates works out to roughly ₱700 per year in added benefit — an amount HPAAC argues many members may conclude isn't worth maintaining regular premium payments for.

Alternative proposed. Strengthen premium collection systems, simplify payment mechanisms, improve enforcement of existing obligations, and push for the full annual National Government appropriation for PhilHealth as mandated by law.

MAIFIP — the non-standardized channel

MAIFIP (Medical Assistance for Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients) is a DOH-administered fund, separate from PhilHealth, and the mechanism underlying Zero Balance Billing at DOH-retained hospitals. It disburses after a PhilHealth case rate is applied, covering the remaining ward-level balance. Administered by the Malasakit Program Office (see DOH Structure).

Disbursement mechanism

MAIFIP disbursement has no case-rate or DRG schedule. Assessment occurs per case, by a hospital's Malasakit Center or medical social worker, with funds applied via guarantee letter — a facility-by-facility, patient-by-patient process rather than a standardized rate. This case-level flexibility is also the property that limits standardized audit and characterizes MAIFIP as a financing channel parallel to, rather than coordinated with, PhilHealth's case-rate system.

Scale and current policy status

Budget: ₱41.16B (2025) → ₱51B (2026). The increase was raised during bicameral conference committee deliberations as a patronage-politics risk by several senators and Church leadership. In December 2025, DOH declined a proposal to redirect MAIFIP funding into PhilHealth, citing PhilHealth's own payment arrears to providers. Revised guidelines (AO 2026-0031, February 2026) set tiered approval authority: CHD directors to ₱1.5M, the Malasakit Program Office cluster head to ₱2M, the Secretary or designee above that threshold.

Presented as mechanism and policy status, not as a recommendation; MAIFIP's financing structure remains an active legislative and administrative question as of this writing.

The UHC pyramid

Four reach-ordered tiers plus one foundation layer, restated from the strategic-focus tags in DOH Structure. The pyramid orders by population reach — Healthy Settings at the base serves the broadest population; Specialty Care at the apex serves the narrowest, highest-acuity population. Healthcare Workforce sits beneath the pyramid as the enabling layer common to all four tiers, not as a fifth reach-ordered tier in its own right.
