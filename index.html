Coverage note. This roster now runs to 92 DOH-retained hospitals plus the 4 GOCC centers — 96 in total, up from the 78 non-GOCC hospitals shown here previously. The increase isn't new construction; it's a better source. DOH's own Health Facility Development Bureau lists more hospitals per region — several established or upgraded as recently as 2025 — than the Zero Balance Billing list this replaces. Of the 92, 13 have a dated, independently verified Medical Center Chief (tiers B/C), plus the Research Institute for Tropical Medicine and the Philippine Orthopedic Center, both already tracked in Internal Structure (the latter confirmed vacant). The remaining 78 are correctly named and located, but their current head could not be confirmed within a defensible source and is marked accordingly.

No DOH-retained hospital in this dataset is licensed Level 1. That tracks with how the system is designed — Level 1 primary hospitals are typically LGU-run, while DOH retains the tertiary and apex tier. What DOH does retain skews toward Level 3 and Specialty (46 of 92, roughly half), with a genuinely large Unspecified column (40 of 92) — the enabling law for a "General Hospital" or "Medical Center" often doesn't state a numeric level at all, so this isn't a research gap so much as a gap in how these laws are written. Levels marked † come from an independent dated source rather than the law itself, and are the minority.

Region XII — SOCCSKSARGEN carries only one DOH-retained hospital in this dataset, against 5–8 in comparably-sized neighboring regions — and it's Unspecified in level. This is the same region whose CHD Director IV post is currently vacant (see Internal Structure).

On hospital-to-CHD distances: every hospital above resolves to a city/province, and every CHD's general location is known, so an approximate distance table is possible in principle — but building one credibly would mean geocoding all 92 facilities against their CHD office coordinates, which hasn't been done here. Happy to build it as a follow-on if useful.

HPAAC objects to the contribution-linked incentive proposal — 8 Jul 2026

Position. HPAAC states it "fully supports PhilHealth's efforts to improve premium collection among direct contributors," but requests, "with all humility," that PhilHealth withdraw the proposal.

Concern 1 — inequity. Two patients with the same illness, receiving the same care in the same hospital, would receive different levels of PhilHealth support — inconsistent, HPAAC argues, with the equity and solidarity principles underpinning the UHC Act.

Concern 2 — counterproductive incentive. Members may come to see premium payment as a voluntary purchase of enhanced benefits rather than a continuing obligation. A 20% increase in all case rates works out to roughly ₱700 per year in added benefit — an amount HPAAC argues many members may conclude isn't worth maintaining regular premium payments for.

Alternative proposed. Strengthen premium collection systems, simplify payment mechanisms, improve enforcement of existing obligations, and push for the full annual National Government appropriation for PhilHealth as mandated by law.
